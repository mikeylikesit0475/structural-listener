# Localisation Error Distribution (Sprint 1.4)

The Sprint 1.4 deliverable: a heat map of localisation error vs. source position on
the 1 m plate, generated by `eval/plot_error_heatmap.py` and written to
`docs/figures/error-heatmap.png`.

![Localisation error heat map](figures/error-heatmap.png)

## Method

`eval/localisation_grid.run_grid_study` injects a crack-growth event at each of 100
positions on a 10\u00d710 grid spanning the plate interior (0.15\u20130.85 m on each axis),
runs the full pipeline (gated envelope-peak picker \u2192 Gauss\u2013Newton
multilateration), and records the per-position error and the solver residual. The
effective wave speed is `c_eff = 1200 m/s` (the grid group-velocity cap, cited in
`docs/sources.md`).

## Result

| Statistic | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Positions localised | 100 / 100 |
| Median error | 2.8 cm |
| Mean error | 3.2 cm |
| 90th percentile | 6.2 cm |
| Max error | 11.3 cm |

The Sprint 1 target (< 5 cm median, BACKLOG.md) is met with comfortable headroom.

## The ADR-006 pattern: edge degradation

The heat map shows higher error near the plate edges, where the direct-to-first-
reflection time gap is smallest. On a 1 m plate with clamped boundaries, the first
reflection from the nearest edge arrives only ~0.3 ms after the direct arrival at an
edge source; the gated picker's 0.35 ms gate can catch the reflection peak instead of
the direct peak, biasing the arrival time and inflating the localisation error.

The mitigation (ADR-006): the multilateration residual is reported as a confidence
score. A reflection-dominated event produces a high residual (geometrically
inconsistent arrival times), and the system reports low confidence rather than a
confidently wrong coordinate. Sprint 1.5 demonstrates this failure case explicitly.

## Why the corners are worst

The four sensors sit at the corners (5 cm inset). A source near a corner is close to
one sensor and far from the opposite corner \u2014 the geometric aperture is large, but
the reflections from the two nearby edges arrive almost simultaneously with the direct
arrival at the near sensor, corrupting its pick. A source near the centre has the
largest direct-to-reflection gap on all four channels and localises best.
